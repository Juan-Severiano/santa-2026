## Decision

The strongest new evidence is **Atakan Aldemir's coordinated multiplier packets**, followed by multi-target block rebuilding. Their published comparisons separate algorithmic gains from newly imported public programs. However, those comparisons start from substantially weaker portfolios, and the bounded factor-packet experiment on our current portfolio found **no improvement**. Do not port the full multiplier-frontier engine on the strength of its old score alone.

The current public leaderboard snapshot still has **ryan at 126,900**, followed by Amin Ebrahimzadeh at 127,069 and c-number at 128,300. This was read through the authenticated official leaderboard API at the beginning of this investigation. No inspected primary source demonstrates a Santa score below 100,000. Reaching 99,999 from 132,542 requires saving 32,543 instructions, approximately 24.6%; none of the observed incremental results establishes that this is attainable. [Official leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/santa-2026/leaderboard).

## Mechanisms worth distinguishing

Line numbers below refer to the decoded source in `atakan-v26-source/`, pinned by the V26 source hash manifest. These mechanisms are materially different from selecting recipes in a fixed existing value universe. [Owning source bundle](https://www.kaggle.com/code/atakanaldemir/santa-2026-joint-recipes-for-large-puzzles?scriptVersionId=371505096).

| File and lines | Actual mechanism | Why it matters / limit |
|---|---|---|
| `elf_multiplier_lanes.cpp:28–101,269–350` | Build an arithmetic atlas and propose factors from existing multipliers, quotients, common divisors, and **new one-write factors**. A factor gets support from feasible quotient/remainder witnesses at several roots. | Generates new integer values. Constructibility and operand independence must be proved; a promising divisor alone is insufficient. |
| `elf_multiplier_lanes.cpp:468–505,509–548,582–628` | Group root rewrites by factor, intern each constructed value once, and evaluate unions of recipes against the complete reachable graph. Keep some individually non-improving partial packets; they may pay off after more roots join. | Avoids adding independent tree costs and avoids pretending an old shared chain disappears while one user still needs it. Packets can span up to seven roots depending on depth. |
| `elf_multiplier_lanes.cpp:555–580,633–648` | Retain physically equal-length graphs, preferring newly reusable product values; continue the search from the changed graph. | Changes the future action set, rather than merely repeating a seed on the same incumbent. The diversity heuristic is not a proof of future savings. |
| `elf_multiplier_frontier.cpp:103–168,172–241` | Generate translated products and near products from congruent differences and recurring divisors, with paid corrections and exact divisions. | Goes beyond `target mod factor` with only small residuals; internal values and their existing chains can provide useful witnesses. |
| `elf_multiplier_frontier.cpp:360–385,388–485` | Combine neutral changes around different factor families. The coalition variant, guarded by `FRONTIER_COALITION`, attempts to retire **every direct consumer** of an old scaffold with a coverage mask and exact union pricing. | The six-part coverage search is implemented in an optional build branch, but source existence is not evidence that it beats the control. See negative controls below. |
| `elf_multiplier_frontier.cpp:615–731` | Graft backward recipe slices from different surviving branches, then jointly evaluate their union. | Uses genuinely different locally generated value graphs. Fixed-universe CP extraction cannot invent the missing values by itself. |
| `elf_interface_closure.cpp:15–108,181–295,327–365` | Pick interfaces by value thresholds, depth and program regions; add paid helpers; close all now-reachable ports; retain a beam by remaining ports and helper count. | Multi-output synthesis charges a shared helper once, and excludes descendants from free inputs. A large number of ports can still produce no completed useful closure. |
| `elf_joint_interfaces.cpp:21–27,38–96` | Join regions and demote ports that have no actual consumer outside the union. | Removes artificial output obligations. The author's corresponding 40-case pilot did not improve its control. |
| `elf_numeric_splice.h:7–52` | Replace definitions globally **by numeric value**, regenerate all real targets, reject cycles, and schedule the whole result. A locally non-improving patch may compete if its values also replace work outside the region. | Distinct from keeping the old definition whenever a new patch happens to produce an already-known value. Final physical instruction count remains decisive. |

The foreign CLI is documented for inspection only. Multiplier lanes take `factors roots width passes depth mode`; frontier takes `frontier_width node_budget packet_width depth style salt [milliseconds]`. Their input block is `id limit ns nt n`, followed by starts, targets and `n` lines `op i j destination`; output includes a statistics header and a complete register program. A Rust implementation should preserve our validated harness protocol instead of adding another unchecked parser. Source entry points: lanes 655–677 and frontier 837–881. [Pinned source](https://www.kaggle.com/code/atakanaldemir/santa-2026-joint-recipes-for-large-puzzles?scriptVersionId=371505096).

## Evidence against repeating attractive but saturated moves

Atakan's internal V30 joint-interface pilot found **zero gains across 40 cases**, despite exposing 9,419 removable ports and 3,098 nodes available only to the combined region. Its relay pilot also found zero gains, despite hundreds of thousands of continuation evaluations. The outside-alias pilot found zero gains. These numbers show that exposing a larger neighborhood or enumerating valid witnesses does not by itself establish useful search coverage. [V26 decoded diagnostic audit](https://www.kaggle.com/code/atakanaldemir/santa-2026-joint-recipes-for-large-puzzles?scriptVersionId=371505096).

The earlier V22 write-up explicitly declines to credit simultaneous numerical coordinates or all-consumer retirement with general superiority. Its decoded coalition development run did save one instruction in 72 cases, while ordinary frontier runs supplied most of the retained improvement. The coordinate test explored hundreds of millions of equation pairs without a retained win. Therefore the coalition mechanism is an experimental candidate, not a demonstrated decisive algorithm. [V22](https://www.kaggle.com/code/atakanaldemir/santa-2026-joint-recipes-for-large-puzzles?scriptVersionId=371469432).

Josecoronel reports a complementary set of negative results: fixed-universe CP extraction had no remaining lower bound below the incumbent on 973 cases; adding helper pairs did not improve a 99-case sample; a hub-swap test found no improving move on ten independent cases. His accumulated-benefit generator reached 150,463 against a 133,790 portfolio, and bounded rollouts remained above the frontier. These are results for that author's implementations and samples; they do not prove global optimality or rule out other candidate generators. [Author's V1 write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/code/josecoronel/santa-2026-everything-i-tried-and-learned?scriptVersionId=372221551).

Our own current-round evidence is similarly limited but reproducible: private Rust LNS improved one of 100 medium cases, 129 → 128 on `scored_0757`, producing 132,542 overall. CP-cross on its eight trajectory portfolios saved zero on those same 100 cases. The baseline, 100 checkpoints and local replay evidence are under `outputs/kaggle-rust-lns/result-v1/` and `outputs/kaggle-rust-lns/cp-local100/`.

## Bounded probes on the current portfolio

Two data diagnostics were completed without running foreign code:

1. **Existing-value retirement:** all 1,000 puzzles, 31,320 non-target helpers with fan-out at least three, and 132,884 direct consumer edges. There are 5,364 consumers with an alternative single-operation recipe whose original operands are independent of the retired pivot, but **zero helpers whose entire consumer set is covered**. This excludes that conservative one-operation move family; it does not exclude multi-operation reconstruction or jointly re-derived known operands. Evidence: `known-value-retirement-diagnostic.json`.
2. **Independent Rust affine-factor packets:** all 1,000 puzzles; 20,345 new-factor families constructible in one or two operations; 11,949 pair-union evaluations; **zero smaller value graphs and zero physical wins**. Each packet tries two roots of `t = f*q ± r`. It keeps at most 40 roots with private cones of at least three writes, uses the 40 smallest known quotient/remainder values up to 65,536, caps 120 families and 300 union evaluations per case, and samples one or two factor proofs. Whole-DAG pricing rejects cycles and pays each new value once. This sampling is not exhaustive two-operation factor synthesis. Runtime: 1.44 seconds wall / 1.15 seconds CPU. Evidence: `factor-packet-probe-report.json`, `factor-packet-probe-cases.json`, `factor_packet_probe.rs`; source SHA-256 `1da5e15423908cc2f7155edf85c009bd27bac988e5814790cfc954b9ba2bd29f`.

**Current implementation decision:** do not add a full factor-lanes port now. A subsequent Rust experiment must change the candidate universe beyond this failed pair menu: for example, three-to-six-output packets with large or newly constructed quotient/remainder values, or new factor witnesses connecting multiple old chains. Keep full reachable-DAG cost, all externally used values, 48-register compilation and exact replay as acceptance gates. Measure new constructed values, complete packets and actual saved instructions separately; a larger witness count is not success. Retain the frozen 132,542 benchmark for that comparison.
